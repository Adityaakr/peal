## 1. What Bonsai is

- Abstract [Paper p.1; identical on ePrint]: "We construct Bonsai, an account-based private payment scheme where validators store a single commitment per account and never store any nullifiers. Instead, each user privately maintains the nullifiers of the payments it has received, and can prune older nullifiers to cold storage so that its active state remains small. An external observer only learns that an account performed some action (send/receive) but never learns the amount or counterparty of a payment."
- Motivation [Paper §1 p.1-2; Blog intro]: "Virtually all deployed private payment systems publish a nullifier for every transaction to prevent double spending. At a million transactions per second, the nullifier set grows by a petabyte each year." The two design requirements are (1) "Succinct validator state. Validator storage may grow with the number of accounts, but not with the number of transactions" and (2) "Offline wallets. Wallets can go offline for arbitrarily long periods and, upon returning, send and receive payments without replaying intervening ledger history."
- Blog intro sizing for 1M TPS with a Groth16 shielded-note design (~200 B/tx, 0.5-1 ms verify): 200 MB/s dissemination, "500-1000 dedicated CPU cores" per validator, nullifier set "a petabyte every year". Context claim: "The Commonware Library now processes ~250K payments per second on Constantinople" (links to github.com/commonwarexyz/monorepo and /constantinople).
- Headline claims [Blog intro bullets]: "each operation has a 256-byte payload and the prototype verifies over a million operations per second on an M5 MacBook Pro (18 cores)"; "validators store a single 32-byte commitment per account and a small number of hashes"; "wallets can go offline indefinitely and resume without replaying intervening chain history".
- Transaction layout [Paper §1 p.2]: "every transaction is 256 bytes: a 32-byte account identifier, the new account commitment, a receipt, a state root, and a 128-byte proof."
- Protocol shape [Paper §2 p.2]: "a tuple of five algorithms (Setup, Create, Register, Send, Receive)". Transfers are two-step: sender posts a receipt via Send; sender privately communicates the receipt opening; receiver runs Receive to claim. Compared to ecash: "reminiscent of ecash [Cha82] style payments but without a centralized intermediary" [Paper §2 p.3].
- Account names are signature verification keys; "Registration proves knowledge of sk_A corresponding to A. After registration, knowledge of the opening of the current account commitment authorizes each state transition." Deployment remark: transfers could be authenticated "with both the signing key (stored securely via threshold/hardware) and the opening of the account commitment (which could be offloaded to a trusted server for proof generation). Thus if the commitment opening is compromised the account does not lose funds – only its privacy." [Paper §6 Overview p.12]

## 2. Exact definitions

### Account commitment
- `com_A = Com_acct(b_A, root_null(A); r_A)` "where b_A is the balance of the account, root_null(A) is the root of the account's local nullifier tree, a sparse Merkle tree (Section 4) keyed by receipt position which contains the nullifiers for all transactions received by A, and r_A is the randomness in the commitment." [Paper §6 p.12]. Blog form: `com = Com_acct(b, root_null; r_A)` to "its balance b and the root root_null of the account's nullifier tree – a sparse Merkle tree keyed by receipt position" [Blog "Our Construction"].
- Registration initialises the tree to the canonical empty root: `com_A = Com_acct(Init[A], root_∅; r_A)` [Paper §6 R_reg p.13; Fig. 3 p.16].

### Receipt
- `ρ = Com_rec(v, Sen, Rec; r'')`, "a hiding commitment ... which binds the amount being sent v, Sender Sen, and Receiver Rec" [Paper §6 p.12]. In the operation-hiding variant `Com_rec` "gains a final type slot": real receipt `Com_rec(v, Sen, Rec, 1; r'')`, dummy `Com_rec(0, ⊥, ⊥, 0; r''')` [Paper §6.1 p.15-16].
- Receipts are appended to a Merkle Mountain Range; `pid` is the receipt's position in the MMR/receipt log [Paper §6 p.12].

### Send relation R_send [Paper §6 p.13, verbatim structure]
- Statement `x_s = (Sen, com, com', ρ)`. Witness `w_s = (b, root_null, r, r', r'', v, Rec)`.
- Constraints: `com = Com_acct(b, root_null; r)`; `com' = Com_acct(b−v, root_null; r')`; `ρ = Com_rec(v, Sen, Rec; r'')`; `Rec ∈ N_λ`; `0 ≤ v ≤ b and b, v, b−v ∈ B`.
- "The receiver need not have registered when the receipt is created, but it must do so before it can receive the transfer."
- Ledger action [Fig. 4 p.17]: verify π, set `Acct[Sen] ← com'`, `MMR.Insert(st_ρ, ρ)`, record new `root_ρ` in `T`. Sender reads off `pid` and forwards `ω = (ρ, pid, v, Sen, Rec, r'')` to the receiver over the private channel.

### Receive relation R_recv [Paper §6 p.14]
- Statement `x_r = (Rec, com, com', root_ρ)`. Witness `w_r = (b, root_null, root'_null, r, r', ρ, pid, π_MMR, π_SMT, v, Sen, r'')`.
- Constraints: `com = Com_acct(b, root_null; r)`; `com' = Com_acct(b+v, root'_null; r')`; `ρ = Com_rec(v, Sen, Rec; r'')`; `MMR.Verify(root_ρ, ρ, pid, π_MMR) = 1`; `SMT.VerifyInsert(root_null, pid, π_SMT) = root'_null`; `v ≥ 0 and b, v, b+v ∈ B`.
- Ledger action [Fig. 4 p.17]: verify π and `root_ρ ∈ T`, then `Acct[Rec] ← com'`. Nothing is appended to the MMR in the base (L_unl) variant [Paper §2 p.3].
- Anchor policy: "Rather than proving knowledge of a validator signature on root_ρ inside the circuit, the receiver reveals the anchor in the clear and the ledger checks it against its own root history, following deployed designs [HBHW26, Pen24, PSS19]" (Zcash, Penumbra, Tornado Cash) [Paper §6 p.13].

### Nullifier handling
- "Nullifiers are never published on chain and are only maintained locally by each user, which is why the position itself can serve as the nullifier." `null = pid` [Paper §2 p.3; §6 p.12].
- "Positions are unique, so distinct receipts always carry distinct nullifiers and no send can block another pending payment. As a by-product, we have additional privacy against the sender who does not learn if/when a send was claimed by the receiver." [Paper §6 p.12]
- Blog gives two benefits of `null = pid`: "the receive circuit is smaller as we avoid having to prove a PRF evaluation to derive the nullifier" and "nullifiers are ordered by when their receipts were created" [Blog "Our Construction"].
- Validators "only maintain: account commitments; the frontier of the receipt Merkle Mountain Range MMR [Tod12]; a set of recent MMR roots T against which they accept receive proofs" [Paper §2 p.3]. Sparse Merkle tree is [DPP16]; `SMT.VerifyInsert` recomputes the root with leaf 0 (non-membership) then leaf 1, costing `2ℓ` hashes [Paper §4 p.7-8].

### Dummy receipts and the operation-hiding relation R_op [Paper §6.1 p.15-16]
- Common record `tx_op = (A, com', ρ, root_ρ, π)`; statement `x = (A, com, com', ρ, root_ρ)`; witness `w = (op, w_op)`, `op ∈ {send, receive}`.
- send branch (`Sen = A`): `com = Com_acct(b, root_null; r)`; `com' = Com_acct(b−v, root_null; r')`; `ρ = Com_rec(v, Sen, Rec, 1; r'')`; `Rec ∈ N_λ`; `0 ≤ v ≤ b`, `b, v, b−v ∈ B`.
- receive branch (`A = Rec`): `com = Com_acct(b, root_null; r)`; `com' = Com_acct(b+v, root'_null; r')`; `ρ_in = Com_rec(v, Sen, Rec, 1; r'')`; `ρ = Com_rec(0, ⊥, ⊥, 0; r''')`; `MMR.Verify(root_ρ, ρ_in, pid, π_MMR) = 1`; `SMT.VerifyInsert(root_null, pid, π_SMT) = root'_null`; `v ≥ 0`, `b, v, b+v ∈ B`. "Else, reject."
- "The branch selector op is part of the witness, so a proof for R_op proves that one valid transition occurred without revealing which branch was taken." "In both branches, the ledger checks root_ρ ∈ T, updates com to com', and inserts ρ into the receipt MMR."
- Why it matters: for the 6-op example the L_unl leakage admits one consistent history; L_ind admits "1, 2, 4, 10, 26, 76 ways" after each op; "hiding the operation type conceals not only the payment links but also how many payments even took place" [Paper §6.1 p.14, Fig. 2 p.15]. Blog: "both send and receive publish the same 256-byte payload (A, com', ρ, root_ρ, π): a 32-byte account identifier, the new account commitment, a receipt, an MMR root, and a 128-byte proof. Every operation appends a receipt to the MMR: a real one for a send and an unspendable dummy for a receive." [Blog "Our Construction"]
- Fees (Remark 1, p.16): "users simply reveal the amount v_fee which becomes a part of the statement in R_send, R_recv, and R_op which additionally prove that the new commitment's balance is reduced by v_fee." Same in Blog.

### Receipt MMR, pruning, cold storage, recent-root policy
- MMR [Tod12]: "appended elements are hashed into the largest possible perfectly balanced Merkle trees"; insertion needs only "a logarithmic number of roots"; "producing an opening proof for an arbitrary element still requires the entire tree" [Paper §4 p.8]. Validators "only store the MMR frontier (a logarithmic number of roots) ... together with the bounded history T" [Paper §6 p.12]. Blog sizing: "With 2^40 receipts, we need at most 40 frontier hashes, or 1.25 KiB with 32-byte hashes. Wallets can claim old receipts by fetching current inclusion proofs from an archive, without replaying the ledger." [Blog "Scaling"]
- Recent-root policy: Setup "initializes the root history T ← {root_ρ}, which retains the W most recently recorded roots" [Paper Fig. 3 p.16]. No value of W is given in either source.
- User-side pruning [Paper §6 p.13]: "For any threshold L, the sparse Merkle tree over positions splits into a prefix [0, L), whose contents are fixed if we never insert below L again, and a suffix [L, ∞). The prefix is summarized by its frontier F_L which is at most ℓ hashes and a user who stores the frontier together with the set S_≥L of positions it has claimed at or above L can produce π_SMT for any position pid ≥ L. The nullifiers below L can be moved to cold storage, from which the full tree, and hence a path for any position, can be recomputed if ever needed. The threshold is chosen by the user and does not affect anything else in the protocol. ... Maintaining the w most recently claimed positions requires a hot state of ℓ + w hashes and supports claiming any receipt at a position above the w-th most recent claim."
- Blog restatement adds: "Older nullifiers can be moved to cold storage, so a receipt below L can still be claimed by retrieving the relevant path from cold storage and updating the frontier. The cold archive still grows with the account's lifetime activity." [Blog "Scaling"]. Paper §1 p.2: "No protocol changes are needed ... Total archived state still grows with lifetime activity."
- Contrast with Tachyon/[BM25]: "Users can be offline, but resuming requires synchronization work by the user or a service that processes the ledger" (blog); in Bonsai "the receiver's saved nullifier state already records which receipts have been claimed and remains valid as unrelated transactions advance the ledger. An archive can supply a current MMR inclusion path" [Paper §3 p.6].

## 3. Remark 2 (verbatim) and the ZK-Pari instantiation

- **Remark 2 (Simulation extractability), Paper §7.2 p.28, verbatim:** "Theorems 4 and 5 establish knowledge soundness and zero knowledge but the payment protocol of Section 6 additionally relies on simulation extractability (Definition 4). We expect that ZK-PARI is simulation extractable, but we defer a formal proof to future work."
- Consequence: Theorems 1 and 2 (security of Π_Bonsai for L_unl and L_ind) assume "Π is a secure NIZK for the R_reg, R_send, and R_recv relations (Definition 4)" where Definition 4 is a *straight-line simulation-extractable* NIZK [Paper §4 p.8-9; Thm 1 p.16; Thm 2 p.22]. The hybrid proof uses straight-line extraction explicitly (H3, H10) [Paper p.20-21]. So the end-to-end security of the prototype instantiation rests on an unproved property of ZK-Pari.
- What is proved for ZK-Pari [Paper §7.2 p.25-28]: Theorem 3 perfect completeness; Theorem 4 knowledge soundness "in the algebraic group model under the (2m+3, 1)-DLOG assumption", knowledge error at most `ε_dlog + (4m+6)/(q−m)`, extractor "straight line: it reads w off the representation of T in the final proof and never rewinds the prover"; Theorem 5 statistical honest-verifier zero knowledge with distance at most `1/(q−m) + m/q`. "The only computational assumption is the following q-type discrete-logarithm assumption." Statements are for "the public-coin variant"; Fiat-Shamir via `ζ := ρ(transcript)` in Fig. 6.
- Construction [Paper §7 p.23-24]: starts from Pari [DMS24] and its improvement [Eag25] (proof `2 G_1 + F`); "neither work provides zero knowledge". Concurrent [KPS26] adds ZK to the older `2 G_1 + 2 F` version. ZK is added by masking the A-side assignment polynomial `ẑ_A := z̄_A + (η_1 + η_2 X) Z_H` with `η_1, η_2 ← F`, and two extra CRS basis elements `a_{k+1} := Z_H(X)`, `a_{k+2} := X Z_H(X)`. Proof `π := (T, U, v_a)`: two G_1 points and one field element. Verification is one equation with three pairings: `e(T, δH) = e(U, τH − ζ·H) · e(v_a·αG + v_R·βG, H)`.
- Batch verification [Paper §2 p.4; §8 p.29]: a batch of B proofs reduces to "three multi-scalar multiplications of size B followed by three pairings [Pol26]" (Pol26 = "The proof is in the pairing", commonware.xyz/blogs/batch-pari, March 2026). Implementation optimisations: verifying key absorbed into the Fiat-Shamir transcript once; Montgomery batch inversion across proofs in the Evaluate phase [Paper §8 p.30].

## 4. Trusted setup, curve, hash, proof size

- Setup is per-index (circuit-specific): `Setup_Pari(1^λ, i) → (crs_i, td)` parses the Square R1CS index `i = (F, n, k, m, H, A, B)`, interpolates the columns of A and B, samples `α, β, δ, τ ← F`, builds keys `Σ_A, Σ_R, Σ_W, Σ_Q` whose exponents embed `a_i(τ), b_i(τ)`; "Set crs_i := (ipk, ivk) and td := (α, β, δ, τ). Publish crs_i and erase td." [Paper Fig. 6 p.24]. The lincheck is "enforced in the instantiation by embedding entries of A and B in the CRS" [Paper §7.1 p.23]. Neither source uses the words "universal" or "updatable", describes a ceremony, or says who ran setup for the benchmarks. Bonsai's own Setup says only "generate and publish the public parameters for commitments and CRS for NIZK relations (if any)" [Fig. 3 p.16]. Groth16 is characterised as having "a circuit specific CRS" [Paper §2 p.4]; the paper does not label ZK-Pari's CRS either way, but Fig. 6 makes it index-dependent.
- Curve: BLS12-381 for all experiments [Paper §8 p.29; Blog "Scalable Private Payments"]. Pedersen hashes are over Jubjub [Paper §8 p.30].
- In-circuit hash: "The circuits only use a collision-resistant hash function" with two benchmarked instantiations: "Pedersen hashes over Jubjub, whose security rests only on the discrete logarithm assumption, and Poseidon over the BLS12-381 scalar field with width 3, α = 5, and 8 full and 57 partial rounds" [Paper §8 p.30]. Both MMR opening and nullifier tree have depth 40 [Paper §8 p.30; Blog].
- Proof size: `2 G_1 + F` = 128 bytes, unchanged from [Eag25] [Paper §7.1 p.23; Table 5 p.31; Blog]. Verification "three pairings, which takes roughly 0.8 milliseconds for every circuit in Table 5" [Paper §8 p.30].
- Constraint system: Square R1CS `(Az)∘(Az) = Bz` [Def. 5 p.9]; "we use a standard compiler from R1CS → SR1CS at the cost of doubling the number of constraints" [Paper §8 p.30]. Blog: "supporting R1CS directly is one opportunity to reduce prover cost."

## 6. Threat model and stated limitations

- Security model [Paper §5 p.9-11]: simulation-based, ideal functionality `F_PrivPay[L]` with interfaces (Create, Register, Send, Receive) plus adversarial `Release`. "The ledger party P_L is always assumed to be honest in our constructions." Theorems 1/2 hold "against static malicious corruption of any set of users". Assumptions: EUF-CMA signatures with deterministic `vk = VKey(sk)`; hiding and binding commitments; simulation-extractable NIZK; collision-resistant hash [Thm 1 p.16].
- Leakage functions [Paper §5 p.10]: `L_unl(Send, S, R, v) = (Send, S)`, `L_unl(Receive, S, R, v) = (Receive, R)` (Sender-Receiver Unlinkability, base protocol, Fig. 4); `L_ind(Send) = (S)`, `L_ind(Receive) = (R)` (Send-Receive Indistinguishability, op-hiding variant, Fig. 5). Registration "always leaks (Register, A)"; account creation "is private and emits no leakage event".
- What the validator/observer sees: the acting account identifier, a new 32-byte commitment, a receipt commitment, an MMR root, a proof [Paper §1 p.2]. "ledger observers see when an account acts, but still cannot see amounts, counterparties, or whether it sends or receives" [Blog intro]. Corrupted sender can withhold a payment's opening ("withheld" status) so "the recipient cannot claim the funds even if they are aware of the incoming transaction" [Paper §5 p.10].
- Privacy weaker than shielded notes, by design: "While this offers weaker on-chain privacy than shielded notes [BCG+14], it allows for a much more efficient solution to nullifier management" [Paper §1 p.2]; blog: "at the cost of less onchain privacy than Zcash" and "ledger indistinguishability in shielded-note systems provides stronger onchain privacy than Bonsai because it also hides which account is acting" [Blog "Scaling", "Privacy Beyond the Ledger"].
- Metadata leakage argument: "when a wallet submits transactions through an RPC node rather than its own node, that RPC node can already link each submitted transaction to the corresponding party" [Paper §1 p.2]; blog widget: "The RPC operator observes transactions 1, 3, and 6 from client A; 2 and 5 from client B; and 4 from client C. The public ledger does not contain these client labels." [Blog "Privacy Beyond the Ledger"]
- Private channel is assumed, not built: "We assume a private channel from the sender to the receiver for simplicity but this can be replaced with an oblivious message routing system such as on-chain encrypted memos [HBHW26], fuzzy message detection [BLMG21] or oblivious message retrieval [LT22]." [Paper §2 p.2]
- Ledger/consensus/storage integration is future work: "The next step is to carry that throughput through networking, consensus, and storage into a complete private payment system." [Blog closing]. The paper's evaluation is the ZK-Pari proof system plus the three circuits; no end-to-end node, network, or consensus measurement is reported [Paper §8].
- Simulation extractability of ZK-Pari is unproved (Remark 2, above).
- Not present in either source (absence, not a statement by the authors): stablecoins or any asset/issuer model (PARScoin appears only in related work [Paper §3 p.5]); a running network, testnet, or deployment; any EVM/bridge integration; published setup parameters or ceremony; recipient discovery/routing implementation; browser or mobile proving (all prover numbers are laptop Rust); selective disclosure, viewing keys, or auditing (auditing is mentioned only for Qurrency/UTT/PARScoin in related work); key recovery or account recovery; fee market or fee values (only Remark 1's mechanism); multi-asset support; concrete value of the root window W or the balance domain B; post-quantum considerations (Qurrency cited as related work only).

## 8. Ideal functionality mechanics (Fig. 1) and proof structure

- `F_PrivPay[L]` keeps `Owner`, `Reg`, `Bal`, and `Log[pid] = (S, R, v, status)`, `status ∈ {withheld, pending, completed}`, "where positions pid are assigned in increasing order. If L = L_ind, the position counter is also incremented on every accepted Receive" (because every op appends one receipt in the op-hiding variant) [Paper Fig. 1 p.11; §6 proof of Thm 2 p.22].
- Send requires `Owner[S] = P, S ∈ Reg, R ∈ N_λ, 0 ≤ v ≤ Bal[S]`, debits, logs at the next `pid` with status `pending` (honest sender) or `withheld` (corrupt sender), outputs `(Sent, pid, R, v)` to the sender and `(pid, S, R, v)` to `Owner[R]` when pending, and broadcasts `L(Send, S, R, v)` [Fig. 1 p.11]. Receive requires `R = R'`, `status = pending`, `Bal[R] + v ∈ B`; credits and marks `completed` [Fig. 1 p.11]. "If R was never created, it has no owner and the payment cannot be claimed." [§5 p.10]
- Simulator sketch [Paper §6 p.16-22]: honest accounts are simulated as dummy commitments `Com_acct(0,0,0; r)` with simulated proofs; honest-to-honest receipts as dummies `Com_rec(0,0,0; r)` (op-hiding: `Com_rec(0,⊥,⊥,0; r)`); corrupt operations are handled by straight-line extraction of the witness and forwarding `Send`/`Receive`/`Release` to the functionality. Hybrids H6/H7 reduce double-claim attempts to MMR binding and SMT insertion binding (hash collisions); H10 reduces forged proofs against honest accounts to commitment hiding+binding via the simulation trapdoor.
- Op-hiding extra hybrid: a receive branch that consumes a receipt published by a receive would require two openings of `Com_rec` (type 0 vs type 1), contradicting binding; "Since the receive branch requires the acting account to equal the receipt's recipient, a corrupted party can never consume a receipt addressed to an honest account." [Paper p.22]

## 9. How the paper positions prior work (§3, p.4-6)

- Ecash variants [Cha82 ... Bra94] "still rely on a global nullifier set that cannot be pruned without expiring coins."
- Credential/signed-account systems (UTT, ZEF, PLATYPUS, PEReDi, PARScoin): "consuming a coin or replacing a credential reveals a serial number or commitment that the authorities must remember to reject reuse." PARScoin is noted for separating the sender's debit from the receiver's credit and "allowing payment initiation while the receiver is offline".
- Shielded note pools (Sander–Ta-Shma, Zerocoin, Zerocash, Qurrency): "The obstacle in these approaches remains the state growth as the published nullifiers accumulate with transaction history."
- Rings/decoys (RingCT, one-out-of-many, Omniring, Zether, Quisquis): validators "still retain the tags needed to detect double spending"; account variants "incur work linear in the chosen anonymity set and require users to retrieve its current state before making a payment."
- Accounts with transfer receipts (BlockMaze, Veksel, DART) are the closest design family: "moving balances into accounts does not itself eliminate transaction-dependent state growth as these systems still publish serial numbers or nullifiers."
- Pruning approaches: coin expiry [Sch98] and short-lived keys (Privacy Pass) "conflict with our goal of allowing the user to be offline indefinitely"; epoch/non-membership-under-every-root schemes [Pol23 Miden, Pal24 Aztec] shift work to users; [BM25] (Bowe–Miers "A note on notes", oblivious synchronization) is the Tachyon reference.
- Verification comparison: Groth16 batch verification "still requires O(B) pairings, each of which costs hundreds of microseconds (amortized)", whereas batched Pari needs three size-B MSMs plus three pairings [Paper §2 p.4].
